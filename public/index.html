ARMS runs on your machine and routes coding agents to the services you already use, with a policy decision on the way out.

What it does

One router.

Plane, GitHub, GitLab, Slack, Postgres, Gmail, S3, Docker, and community tool servers share this gateway.

Policy per agent.

Turn a connector, or one tool inside it, on or off for a single harness or for every harness.

A map of the calls.

See which harness may reach which tool, and read the verdict in the policy audit.

Control plane

Harnesses.

Clients on stdio, HTTP, and SSE appear as they connect. You do not type a client id.

Inventory.

Native connectors and the community servers you register stay listed across restarts.

Connection map.

Each cell is a harness, a tool, the transport, and whether policy allows the call.

Before a call goes out

Approvals.

A queued tool call or trust decision waits for a yes or a no, and the reviewer is recorded.

Read-only mode.

A harness can be barred from create, update, and delete calls. The block is written to the audit.

From the chat.

A connected agent can ask for status, switch a connector, or set a harness policy.

Native connectors

Repositories, pull requests, issues, and workflows.

Projects, issues, merge requests, and files.

Slack

Postgres

Strings, hashes, keys, and cache stats.

S3

Buckets, objects, and metadata.

Docker

Containers, images, and logs.

Still on your machine

Community servers.

Register an npx or uvx tool server and ARMS runs it under a namespace you name.

Encrypted vault.

API keys and tokens are stored with AES-GCM. The vault key stays with you.

On this machine.

Diagnostics, rate limits, cache, telemetry, and alerts live in the local control plane.
